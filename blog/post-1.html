How to Build a Knowledge Base Your Team Will Actually Use

October 1, 2026 · By the NovaDocs Team · 5 min read

Knowledge bases fail for a predictable reason: they're built for the writer, not the reader. The person who writes the documentation already knows the answer; the person who needs to find it doesn't know what to search for.

Start with the questions, not the answers

Before writing a single document, collect the top 20 questions your team gets asked repeatedly. Support tickets, Slack messages, and onboarding notes are a goldmine. Write your knowledge base to answer those questions first.

Use the same words your readers use

If your users call it a "workspace" but your docs call it a "project", nobody will find anything. Run a quick card-sorting exercise to learn what vocabulary your team uses naturally.

Assign an owner to every page

Documents without owners go stale. Assign each page to someone who uses that feature daily. Their job is to update the page whenever the feature changes — not to be a professional writer.

Make it easy to flag outdated content

Add a "Was this helpful? No → Tell us why" button. Readers who find outdated content will tell you if you make it frictionless. NovaDocs shows a "Flag as outdated" button on every page.

Review quarterly, not annually

Set a recurring calendar event: "Review and prune the knowledge base." Delete pages that haven't been viewed in 90 days. Update anything with a stale date. Four reviews a year beats one big annual overhaul.
